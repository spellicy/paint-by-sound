# Concept: Paint by Sound

*Originally conceived by Stephen Spellicy, July 2023, as a creativity
exercise.*

## The idea

Paint by Sound is a concept for a new artform and gallery exhibit: a
robotic arm, equipped with a supply of paint and a wide color palette,
that physically paints an abstract, modern-art canvas live &mdash; driven
not by a human hand but by music. An AI program listens to a piece of
music as it plays and translates what it hears directly into the robot's
movements and color choices, so the resulting painting is a physical
artifact of that specific piece of music, generated as it is heard.

The inspiration is a quote from Wassily Kandinsky: "the eyes are made for
astronomy, the ears for harmony, and these are sister sciences." Kandinsky
treated color and sound as two expressions of the same underlying
language, and painted with musical terms like "composition" and
"improvisation" in mind. Paint by Sound takes that connection literally,
using music as an active, real-time input to a visual, physical process.
The concept also draws on the author's own background studying
composition and film scoring at Berklee College of Music before moving
into technology &mdash; a personal bridge between the two "sister
sciences" the exhibit is built around.

## How the AI listens

The program doesn't just react to volume. It's designed to interpret music
across several dimensions at once, each feeding into a different aspect
of the robot's brushwork:

- **Pitch and the chromatic scale** &mdash; individual notes, across
  multiple octaves, map to specific colors and shades, so the same note
  consistently suggests the same region of the palette.
- **Mode and key** &mdash; the overall tonal color of a piece (major vs.
  minor, and beyond) shifts the palette's mood.
- **Rhythm and tempo** &mdash; drive the pace and energy of the arm's
  movement across the canvas.
- **Note duration** &mdash; sustained notes versus short, percussive ones
  produce correspondingly different brush gestures.
- **Melody and harmony** &mdash; the interplay of a lead line against
  supporting harmony informs composition and layering on the canvas, not
  just isolated points of color.

Source material could be a solo instrumental performance, a full band, or
an orchestra &mdash; anything from a Jimi Hendrix guitar solo to a John
Coltrane melody. Different exhibits could be themed by artist, by
technique or style (a Kandinsky-style exhibit alongside a Pollock-style
one), or by musical era, letting the same piece of music be reinterpreted
through different visual languages.

## The gallery experience

Beyond the live painting itself, the concept calls for the gallery room to
also display the exhibit's past work: finished paintings hung alongside
audio narration explaining the musical inspiration behind each piece.
That narration is meant to close the loop for the visitor &mdash; not just
showing what the music produced, but explaining why, so the connection
between the original musical inspiration and the resulting physical
artwork is fully legible. The goal is a true multimodal artform: sound,
translated live into a physical, visual object, with the reasoning behind
that translation made explicit rather than left for the viewer to guess.

## Open questions

The concept doesn't shy away from the obvious tension it raises: AI-generated
art competing in a market built around human authorship. An exhibit like
this invites real questions about how it affects the market for human
artists, and about the authenticity of art created by a machine
interpreting another artist's music rather than by a human hand. Paint by
Sound is offered as a creative exploration of what that kind of art could
look like, not as an argument that it should replace human-made work.
